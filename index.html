A SHORT ORIENTATION

Think about readiness for your assigned setting

You are advising on AI readiness for a country or U.S. state: the ability to support useful AI activity through energy, infrastructure, people, resources, and public support. Use the numbers to examine tradeoffs, then make your own judgment.

AI levels describe scale. Low, medium, and high are the course’s power-demand categories. Counts represent a mix of these demand units across your assigned setting; you are estimating needs, not designing facilities.

MW measures power. It describes a rate of electricity demand or rated supply. A technology’s nameplate MW is its rated capacity; it does not say how much dependable electricity it can deliver.

Ranges invite comparison. Capacity, investment, and time ranges come from our course apps. They are teaching references, not location forecasts or quoted prices.

Use the setting and location assigned in the simulation. Each location keeps its own scenario and advice. Course reference ranges are the same for every location; selection does not supply local statistics or a readiness score.

      What else would an annual energy or cost estimate need?

      Power is not annual energy

      Actual AI electricity use needs operating hours and utilization. Annual generation needs output over time, including capacity factors. The course apps do not supply those inputs.

      Location totals and annual costs

      An electricity share needs a matching electricity total and year for your assigned setting. Annual electricity costs need energy use and an appropriate electricity price. Dependable supply, emissions, and water totals need further operating and resource data.

      These results are marked not provided by the course apps. No local electricity totals, prices, capacity factors, or environmental rates have been added.

      A NEW OPTION ENTERS THE DISCUSSION

      Would an SMR change your advice?

      For this simulation: in 2027, a selected small modular reactor (SMR) design completes demonstration testing and receives U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission design certification.

      An SMR is a nuclear energy option. Consider whether this hypothetical milestone helps with the 2030 target, belongs in a later recommendation, or does not fit your energy mix.

      What does the milestone change in this explorer?

      The milestone is a story premise from the course challenge. It does not change the calculator’s 4–7-year course SMR range or establish that electricity will be available by 2030. Financing, resources, infrastructure, and the applicable approvals for your assigned setting remain part of your judgment.
